% ===================== 2. Motivation =====================
\begin{frame}{Motivation}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.58\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\itemsep=0.7em
        \item Beta testing is resource-intensive: balancing a game before release takes many diverse human testers over many sessions, and still cannot cover the full design space.
        \item Game designers face combinatorial design spaces that human playtesting cannot cover.
        \item A single trained agent (RL or LLM) used as an oracle collapses design quality to one playstyle's quirks.
        \item What is needed: an automated, reproducible way to evaluate each candidate design against a diverse population of strategies rather than a single oracle.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.4\textwidth}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{board_default.png}\\
      \tiny Monopoly: our testbed
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

% ===================== 3. The problem, and why it is hard =====================
\begin{frame}{The problem, and why it is hard}
  \begin{itemize}\itemsep=0.7em
    \item \textbf{The core question:} how can we accelerate beta testing while ensuring that every playstyle enjoys the game, across a combination of metrics?
    \item \textbf{Prior automated playtesting evaluates with a single agent class:}
      \begin{itemize}\itemsep=0.2em
        \item a single trained oracle (AlphaStar-style league play, or one LLM player model),
        \item a pool of agents / \emph{procedural personas} standing in for distinct playstyles,
        \item content generation scored by a single fitness signal.
      \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Why they fall short:} one agent class cannot detect its own blind spots; even a diverse pool can rate a design fair while a different class sees it as broken.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ===================== 4. Our approach =====================
\begin{frame}{Our approach, and why it is better}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.6\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\itemsep=0.55em
        \item \textbf{Closed loop:} a genetic algorithm searches a 66-dim board space (per-property cost $\times$ rent multipliers + a 22-bit keep-mask) to minimise a 5-axis composite objective.
        \item \textbf{Evaluate against a fixed 30-strategy pool} (10 archetypes + 20 sampled), with shared random numbers, so an unchanged design gives byte-identical metrics.
        \item \textbf{Cross-class check:} re-evaluate the winners with an \emph{independent} agent class (Qwen2.5-1.5B, deterministic STATE/ECHO/REASON/ANSWER validation).
        \item \textbf{Why it is better:} no single evaluator can see its own blind spots; two independent classes can. Agreement builds trust; disagreement is the diagnostic.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.4\textwidth}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{board_ga_2p_mask.png}\\
      \tiny 2-player GA winner (vs.\ default)\\[0.4em]
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{board_ga_3p_mask.png}\\
      \tiny 3-player GA winner (vs.\ default)
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

% ===================== 5. The objective =====================
\begin{frame}{The objective: one composite, five design qualities}
  \[
    \text{score}(\theta) \;=\; w_F\,\overline{F} \;+\; w_{F_{\max}}\,F_{\max}
    \;+\; w_R\,\tfrac{|\overline{R}-R^*|}{R^*} \;+\; w_D\,\overline{D}
    \;+\; w_T\,\tfrac{|\overline{T}-T^*|}{T^*}
  \]
  \vspace{0.2em}
  \begin{center}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    Term & Measures & Goal \\
    \midrule
    $\overline{F}$ mean fairness     & win-rate spread between players (avg.\ over matchups) & minimise \\
    $F_{\max}$ worst-pair fairness   & the \emph{worst} matchup's spread (robustness)        & minimise \\
    $\overline{R}$ length            & mean rounds per game                                  & target $60$ \\
    $\overline{D}$ draw rate         & fraction of games with no winner                      & minimise \\
    $\overline{T}$ transfer          & inter-player money moved per round                    & target $100$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item Weights $w=(1,\,0.5,\,0.5,\,0.3,\,0.3)$ are fixed; zeroing terms one at a time gives the single-objective ablations.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ===================== 6. Results: rule-based GA =====================
\begin{frame}{Results: the GA finds better boards}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]

        \item \textbf{$+13\%$} over a random-search baseline at a matched $\sim$842-evaluation budget.

% ===================== 8. Results: the cross-class check =====================
\begin{frame}{Results: the cross-class check}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\itemsep=0.7em
        \item \textbf{Validation:} re-evaluated by the independent LLM class (Qwen2.5-1.5B), the GA winner boards show the same pacing and interaction effects as the strategy pool (shorter games, more transfer).
        \item \textbf{The diagnostic:} in contrast, a board optimised \emph{by} the LLM looks fair to the LLM but performs consistently worse under the strategy pool: fairness gap $0.20$ vs.\ $0.379$ at 2p, $0.05$ vs.\ $0.404$ at 3p.
        \item We need \emph{both} generalist (diverse-pool) and player-mode-specific (LLM) feedback to guide our designs.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}

% ===================== 15. Takeaway =====================
\begin{frame}{Takeaway}
  \begin{itemize}\itemsep=0.8em
    \item A diverse strategy pool beats a single oracle as an evaluator for game-design optimisation.
    \item Cross-class probing turns evaluator disagreement into a blind-spot diagnostic you cannot get from one class.
    \item Variance reduction (shared random numbers) lets a small search budget see real signal; the bottleneck is problem framing, not optimiser cleverness.
    \item The recipe transfers to any parameterised multi-agent system: market mechanisms, recommendation systems, RL environment design.
  \end{itemize}
